## New

- **Passkeys from iCloud Keychain.** Apple granted Ardia the browser passkey entitlement, so sites can offer the passkeys saved in your iCloud Keychain, the same ones Safari and your iPhone use. When a site saves a passkey you choose Ardia's own passkeys or Passwords (iCloud Keychain), and the first time a site uses them, macOS asks whether Ardia may. Settings › Engines › Passkeys shows the status and opens System Settings if it's off. Security keys, phone sign-in and Touch ID passkeys work as before.
- **iCloud Passwords works in Ardia.** Apple approved Ardia, so Apple's iCloud Passwords extension can now reach the Passwords app: add it from the Chrome Web Store and macOS asks to enable Password AutoFill for Ardia. The Apple Passwords plugin is gone. If you used it, Ardia's own passwords take over again, with Offer to save passwords turned back on.
- **Pages in WebKit get what Chromium pages have.** A site that opens in WebKit now fills your saved passwords, addresses and cards (only on the site they belong to), offers to save a sign-in after it succeeds, shows its notifications through Ardia, blocks ads and trackers as strictly as Chromium does (your own uBlock Origin filters included), refuses certificate errors it can't safely bypass and honours HSTS, and has find, view source, save page and Developer Tools.
- **Your sign-in comes along when a site switches engines.** Moving a site between Chromium and WebKit carries its cookies and saved site data both ways, so you stay signed in. Private windows and sites that block cookies carry nothing. Settings › Advanced › Bring sign-ins along turns it off.
- **Plugins can add more to the browser.** A plugin can now add commands to the command bar, a view in the sidebar, a panel beside the page, a toolbar button with a popover and an address-bar keyword, all drawn in Ardia's own style. Plugins still arrive off and ask first. The consent sheet lists each permission separately, including the exact websites a plugin may reach, and plugins never run in private windows. Mac only for now.
- **The profile switcher is one switch.** Settings › Profiles › "Show the profile switcher in the sidebar" replaces Dots, Icons and Nothing. Each profile is a dot unless you gave it an icon: right-click a dot › Add Icon…, or Settings › Profiles › Icon, and Remove turns it back into a dot. Onboarding no longer asks "Dots or icons?". Nothing changes on update: a switcher that showed dots, icons or nothing still does.
- **Edit any theme colour.** Pick one of the nine colours in Settings › Profiles › Theme, the New Tab page's Make it yours panel or onboarding, and the editor opens on it. Your changes stay with that colour when you switch away and back, and Reset puts it back as it came. The separate Custom swatch is gone: a custom theme you already had becomes your edit of the colour it was on, and looks exactly as before.
- **Picture choices in Settings.** Settings › Appearance › Switching tabs shows what ⌃Tab does as two cards, Step through tabs and Recent tabs switcher, so people coming from Arc can get the switcher back in one click. If your own shortcuts match neither, it says Custom and links to them. The address bar's place, what ⌘L opens and hiding the toolbar while scrolling are picture cards too.
- **One switch for programs that control Ardia.** Settings › Advanced › Command line and automation has "Let programs control Ardia", for the `ardia` command and any plugin that drives the browser. Turning it off stays off: a restart, or installing a plugin that wants control, leaves it off, and the plugin tells you where to turn it on. The Command Line plugin is now an ordinary plugin, using the same extension points any plugin can.

## Fixed

- **Hide Element can no longer be started or steered by a page; rules it saved are checked on update.**
- **Shortcuts keep working after you leave a password field.** Switching to another app while a web page's password field had focus could leave macOS's secure keyboard input on, so global shortcuts in the next app didn't work until you came back.
- **The command bar opens at its full size.** Clicking the address field or pressing ⌘L shows the whole panel on the first frame. It used to grow out of the field over about 150 ms, uncovering its rows one at a time.
- **Tabs on top: groups open and close in one move.** Opening or closing a group in the top strip could make its tabs jitter, overlap, or drop into the open tabs menu and back while it slid, and a tab selected inside a closed group was drawn over its neighbour for a few frames.
- **No crash at launch after updating from a very old Ardia.** Profiles carried over from 0.2.19 or older could crash on their first launches while Ardia moved their bookmarks into Chrome's. It now waits until Chrome's bookmarks have loaded.
- **The Dock keeps the app icon you picked when a download starts.** With Midnight, Noir or another icon picked, the first download of a session put the default icon back in the Dock until Ardia restarted.
- **Back to Tab brings Ardia forward.** With another app in front, clicking Back to Tab on a video's picture-in-picture window now brings Ardia and the video's tab to the front. It used to close the player and leave Ardia behind the other app.
- **More streaming sites play.** YouTube TV, Canal+, myCANAL, Audible's cloud player, OSN+ and Anghami now open in WebKit from the first visit, where their players work. Thanks, [@jonnyasmar](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/2553), [@edigeh](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/2171) and [@1homsi](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/1419).
- **An address with a space after its host opens.** Typing something like `example.com /docs` in the command bar opens the address when it can't be a search phrase; otherwise it's offered right under the search. Thanks, [@Pruthvirajcodes](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/2490).
- **Imported bookmark files keep their folders.** Importing a bookmarks HTML file kept the bookmarks bar but dropped the name of every other top-level folder and left its links loose in Other Bookmarks. Folders now come over as they were, empty ones included. Thanks, [@Pruthvirajcodes](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/2489).
- **Links stay in Little Ardia.** A link a page opens with a scripted click now opens in the little window instead of jumping to a full window.
- **A tucked picture-in-picture player comes back when you click its arrow**, on either edge of the screen. The click used to start a window drag instead. Thanks, [@skap3214](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/2844).

## Smaller

- Hide toolbar while scrolling is off by default; if you turned it on, it stays on.
- The `ardia` command (Command Line 0.6.0) is one program with no Python inside, and every command but `ardia tabs` starts faster. `ardia browse --json` puts each error on stdout as one JSON line with a `code`, and `batch` keeps the quotes in `eval` lines. Commands it runs after an update stop when Ardia quits.
- The Command Line plugin is no longer packed inside Ardia: it downloads when you install it, checked against the copy this Ardia was released with, so the app is smaller. If you already use it, Settings › Plugins offers Update… for 0.6.0, and your current `ardia` keeps working until you accept.
- Pinching or dragging an edge of the picture-in-picture player resizes it smoothly, following every frame of the display, even while another app is in front, and a player parked partly past the screen edge resizes where it is instead of jumping back on screen. Thanks, [@luangjokaj](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/3345).
- The floating sidebar you get by hovering a hidden sidebar lines up with the page's frame at every thickness, corners included.
- `ardia events` sends `tab.idle` once per idle stretch, even as other programs start and stop listening. Thanks, [@Pruthvirajcodes](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/3231).
- On Linux, Ardia updates itself, and the page no longer flashes when the hidden toolbar opens or folds away.
- The compass in the engine badge sits in the middle of its plate. Thanks, [@luangjokaj](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/pull/3229).

## Known issues

- In pages that open in WebKit, a site's background notifications (sent by its service worker) arrive only when every profile that uses WebKit allows notifications for that site. Notifications from the open page itself aren't affected.
- Switching tabs sets a few more timers than Ardia's performance budget allows. You won't notice it, and the next update brings it back under budget ([#3449](https://github.com/ardia-browser/ardia/issues/3449)).
